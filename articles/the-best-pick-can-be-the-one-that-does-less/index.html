The Best Pick Can Be the One That Does Less

Compare two picks by the useful completions they preserve and the immediate strength those choices cost.

HermodLabs / 5 min read / Articles

In this field guide

  1. Value the Next Decisions
  2. Name the Completions You Would Use
  3. Identify the Reveal That Could Matter
  4. Check That the Choice Survives Until You Use It
  5. Change What the Opponent Has Revealed
  6. Compare the Futures Before Picking

Two candidates fit your next pick. One strengthens your preferred fight immediately; the other leaves a useful response to an enemy reveal. Is that future choice worth the strength you give up now? This article shows how to compare candidates through the credible lineups they preserve. We will price one immediate tradeoff, identify the reveal that could repay it, and remove an assumption to see when the focused pick becomes preferable.

Value the Next Decisions

Suppose your team has Maokai Jungle, Orianna Mid, and Jinx Bottom. Top and Support remain open. In this preparation exercise, you choose Top now, see the opponent's remaining picks, and then choose Support. The candidates are Ornn and Gragas. Assume both Top picks are comfortable for your player. Your team's preparation favors Ornn for a sustained frontal fight, while Gragas supports a short catch-and-reposition sequence that the team has also practiced.

The comparison has two parts: what each pick contributes to the current plan, and what useful choice remains for Support after the reveal. The ability to make that later choice is optionality. Its value depends on finished teams. A possible dive, pick, or sidelane label needs legal champions, available roles, and players who can execute the route before it belongs in the comparison.

Name the Completions You Would Use

For this exercise, the team has prepared three acceptable completions:

  • Ornn with Lulu completes its preferred front-to-back lineup.
  • Gragas with Lulu preserves a front-to-back lineup, with less of the sustained frontline strength the team wants in this matchup.
  • Gragas with Nautilus supports a practiced catch route against an opponent whose rotations expose a reachable target.

The third completion needs a full sequence. Maokai holds space while Gragas and Nautilus create a short opening; Orianna and Jinx remain close enough to follow. The team must be able to secure the catch without leaving its own carry exposed. Those conditions are part of accepting the lineup. These are the roster's assumptions for this example. Other teams may have different Ornn completions or may be unable to execute the Gragas route at all.

We exclude an unpracticed split-push branch. We also exclude Gragas's potential Jungle assignment because Jungle is already occupied. Neither provides a remaining choice in this decision.

Identify the Reveal That Could Matter

Suppose the hidden enemy picks could complete either a short-range frontal team or a team that can repeatedly disengage our approach but is vulnerable while rotating. Against the first response, our team prefers the Ornn–Lulu completion. It gives up some of that preferred fight strength by choosing Gragas now.

Against the second, the prepared Gragas–Nautilus catch route becomes more attractive. The later Support decision lets the team use the reveal: choose Lulu for the frontal contest or Nautilus for the reachable rotation target.

That is the benefit being purchased. Gragas leaves an acceptable frontal fallback and a second completion that answers a plausible enemy direction. Under these assumptions, the team chooses Gragas because it considers the second response likely enough, and the catch completion useful enough, to justify the immediate cost.

The reasoning can remain qualitative. Record the expected responses and the reason the team accepts the tradeoff; a numerical flexibility score would require evidence about their likelihood and value.

Check That the Choice Survives Until You Use It

A future completion must remain available through the decisions before your next pick. If the opponent can remove Nautilus and the team has no acceptable substitute, the catch branch is fragile. Account for that response before crediting Gragas with a reliable later choice. Player pools matter in the same way. If Support is uncomfortable on the required catch pick, the branch needs practice before it earns weight in this draft comparison.

Other costs belong beside those checks: a worse lane matchup, weaker immediate synergy, or a frontline requirement that remains unfinished. Our example accepts the Gragas–Lulu fallback. If that lineup cannot hold enough space for Jinx in the expected fight, preserving the catch route comes at a much larger price.

Flexibility is therefore specific to the draft and roster. A Top/Jungle flex would provide a different benefit while both roles remained open. Here, the available choice concerns the final Support and the resulting fight plan.

Change What the Opponent Has Revealed

Now suppose the opponent has already committed to the short-range frontal team. There is little remaining information that would make the catch completion our preferred answer. With the same player pools and available champions, the team chooses Ornn. His stronger contribution to the relevant fight outweighs a later branch the team has little reason to use.

The decision also shifts toward Ornn if Nautilus becomes unavailable with no practiced replacement. In that case, Gragas's broader possibilities no longer include the particular completion that justified the tradeoff.

These changes expose the useful comparison: the quality of the alternatives, the likelihood of wanting them, and the immediate cost of keeping them. Commitment timing adds another question: how long can those choices remain open? The companion article explores that question.

Compare the Futures Before Picking

  1. What does each candidate improve now, and what strength would we give up?
  2. Which complete lineups can our players still build after likely enemy responses?
  3. What upcoming reveal would make us choose a preserved alternative?
  4. Is that alternative useful and reachable enough to justify its immediate cost?
