A philosophy of warranted strategic confidence

A champion can possess a useful mechanic without making a strategy dependable. Before a team builds around that contribution, it should ask what the evidence establishes, which conditions remain unresolved, and how much confidence the claim deserves.

Our positionStrategic confidence should rise as evidence strengthens and required conditions are satisfied.
The problem

“We have it” can hide several different claims.

Players often say that a composition has engage, protection, damage, or disengage. The language is useful because it is quick. It becomes dangerous when possession of a mechanic is treated as proof that a strategic job is solved.

An ability may resemble the tool we need. Its effects may genuinely contribute to the job. Yet its range, timing, target, geometry, setup, or follow-through may still prevent the contribution from becoming usable in the intended pathway.

Possibility tells us where to look. Evidence tells us what we may responsibly rely on.

A League example

Rakan has a knockup. Is initiation solved?

The answer changes as we make more of the hidden claim explicit.

Observed actionGrand Entrance knocks enemies up.

This describes a mechanic in Rakan's kit.

○
→
Strategic relevanceThe knockup can help initiate.

A rule connects the effect to a team-level job.

→
Required situationRakan must establish usable access.

Range, geometry, timing, vision, and enemy interruption qualify the claim.

→
Warranted conclusionInitiation is conditionally supplied.

The possibility is real, but the unresolved conditions should remain visible.

What we should not say yet

“Rakan solves initiation.” That sentence erases the difference between owning a tool, reaching the target, surviving interference, and receiving coordinated follow-through.

A place for uncertainty

Unknown is neither false nor true.

A model becomes dishonest when it forces incomplete evidence into a yes-or-no answer.

Too pessimistic

“The condition is unrecorded, so the contribution does not exist.”

This throws away a defensible strategic relationship merely because the situation has not been resolved.

Our position

“The contribution exists, but whether we can rely on it remains conditional.”

This preserves both the relevant possibility and the reason confidence must remain limited.

Too confident

“The champion has the mechanic, so the requirement is covered.”

This turns missing information into permission to claim that the pathway is ready.

Degrees of warranted commitment

Support should become stronger only when its justification becomes stronger.

These categories provide an explicit vocabulary for keeping different evidential claims apart.

01None

No supported contribution is available for the required job.

02Trace

A relevant evidence chain exists, but it does not yet establish usable supply.

03Conditional

The contribution is modeled, but one or more facts needed to rely on it remain unresolved.

04Confirmed

The modeled conditions are satisfied, or the contribution does not depend on an unresolved condition.

Conditional support is a meaningful finding.

It is often the most faithful answer available before a fight occurs. Keeping uncertainty visible is part of a faithful analysis.

Structure before averages

A pathway is not a bag of interchangeable strengths.

Strategic jobs are connected in an order. Later strength cannot always compensate for an earlier step that never happens.

ACCESSAbsent

The team cannot reliably reach the target.

→
CONTROLConfirmed

Control exists if contact occurs.

→
DAMAGEConfirmed

Damage exists if a target is held.

→
CONVERTConditional

Conversion remains possible afterward.

The route still cannot begin.

A compact summary may describe the evidence across all four jobs, but it must not conceal the absent access step. Players should inspect the sequence to check whether every required stage is supported.

The honesty required by measurement

A useful summary is still a constructed judgment.

Putting ordered evidence into a common scale makes comparison easier. It does not turn strategic readiness into a physical quantity.

Order does not prove equal distance

Confirmed is stronger than Conditional, but we have not proven that the difference between them has the same gameplay value as the difference between Trace and None.

Requirements are not always equally decisive

One missing access step may matter more than several well-supported downstream jobs. Equal treatment is an explicit default that can be revised as evidence improves.

Strength cannot always compensate for absence

A confirmed damage contribution does not repair a pathway that cannot establish contact. Aggregation must remain subordinate to the ordered structure.

The model cannot grade what it does not contain

Patch changes, execution, undocumented interactions, and missing conditions may make practical readiness different from modeled readiness.

Responsible claim

“This pathway has stronger modeled supply evidence than the alternative under the same definitions and conditions.”

Claim we reject

“This strategy is 67% likely to work,” or “a score of 80 is twice as strategically ready as 40.”

Keep distinct questions distinct

Readiness is not every good thing at once.

ReadinessHow strong is the evidence for each required job?

Use the strongest supported contribution to describe the maturity of that job.

RobustnessWhat remains when a provider fails?

A second independent provider can make the pathway less fragile even when its readiness remains unchanged.

Strategic valueIs this the right pathway to pursue?

A well-supported plan can still be inappropriate for the enemy, the players, or the objective.

OutcomeWill the team win?

No supply assessment can promise execution, adaptation, or victory.

Use the language to reason

Ask which strategic relationship changed.

The point of a summary is to direct attention back toward an inspectable strategic claim.

01
Locate the job

Which pathway requirement gained or lost support?

Name the job before evaluating the champion. “Target Access became Conditional” says more than “the composition improved.”

02
Inspect the evidence

Which champion action supports it?

Follow the claim back to the mechanic, effect, and relationship that justify the contribution.

03
Name the condition

What must be true for the contribution to matter?

Range, geometry, timing, target state, information, coordination, and enemy pressure should remain visible.

04
Make the judgment

Is that evidence sufficient for this team to rely on?

The model can organize the reasons. Players still decide whether the uncertainty and tradeoffs are acceptable.

How this position shapes Hermod

Make confidence answerable to evidence.

This philosophy motivates our distinction between None, Trace, Conditional, and Confirmed supply. It also motivates the Supply Readiness Index as a compact comparison aid. Its interpretation depends on the underlying pathway; it measures neither win probability nor overall champion strength.

The categories and their summary remain modeling choices. They should be inspectable, revisable, and judged by whether they help players make better distinctions.

We would rather show that a strategy is conditionally supported than turn incomplete evidence into confident advice.
