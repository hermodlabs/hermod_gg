Our methodology

Hermod does not begin from the belief that we already possess the correct theory of League of Legends. We study how players explain strategy, preserve the claims they make, consolidate recurring relationships, and make our own abstractions visible enough to challenge.

Our standardCommunity-grounded does not mean community-certified. It means the source, transformation, interpretation, uncertainty, and opportunity for revision should remain visible.
The honest claim

We study community knowledge. We do not claim to speak for the community.

Players, coaches, analysts, creators, and theorycrafters already describe strategic relationships every day. They explain why an engage failed, why a carry could not be protected, why a composition needed another entrance, or why a patch changed more than a champion’s damage. This knowledge appears in guides, discussions, videos, reviews, and ordinary arguments about the game.

Hermod tries to make recurring ideas explicit and comparable. Reading community-authored material is community research; it is not the same as interviewing representative players or receiving community approval. A subreddit, creator audience, professional team, rank, or region is not the whole League community.

Our goal is not to turn community language into proof. It is to turn useful claims into something people can inspect, compare, test, and revise.

Where terms come from

Not every word in the model has the same origin.

Calling every term “community language” would hide the work of interpretation. We distinguish vocabulary we inherit from vocabulary we introduce.

01Community term

Language players already use

Terms such as “front-to-back,” “peel,” “pick,” and “dive” arrive with histories, disagreements, and different meanings across communities.

02Community-grounded abstraction

A reusable term for recurring claims

A phrase such as Target Access may consolidate many descriptions of reaching a relevant champion or space. Hermod created the abstraction; community examples ground its purpose.

03Analyst interpretation

A deliberately established boundary

Deciding that several pathways belong to Protect Hypercarry is a semantic judgment. Structure can support the decision without forcing it.

04Normative choice

A declared strategic value

Saying that preserving a carry matters more than retaining another initiation route expresses a preference, not a mechanical fact.

Our obligation

When Hermod introduces a term or boundary, it should not borrow the authority of “the community” to make the choice appear inevitable.

The working method

Preserve the source. Separate the transformations. Show the judgment.

The process is designed to prevent a persuasive comment from becoming an unexplained strategic truth.

1Define

Ask a bounded question

Begin with a specific inquiry, such as how players describe compositions that preserve one critical damage source.

2Collect

Record source context

Retain the source, date, patch, community, game context, and competitive level when known.

3Extract

Break prose into atomic claims

Separate claims about mechanics, conditions, causal relations, categories, preferences, and predictions.

4Code

Connect claims to explicit concepts

Identify effects, basic capabilities, conditions, strategic capabilities, dependencies, and possible exposures.

5Compare

Look for recurring relationships

Search across independent sources for enables, requires, precedes, interrupts, and substitutes-for relationships.

6Challenge

Seek disagreement and counterexamples

Look for reversed sequences, missing stages, alternate routes, patch dependence, and meanings that vary by community.

7Interpret

Curate boundaries deliberately

Create candidate pathways and archetypes while recording where consolidation ends and semantic judgment begins.

8Revise

Test, review, and version

Check mechanics, compare additional cases, invite criticism, and update conclusions when the game or evidence changes.

The second conclusion requires a boundary decision.

A community may separate front-to-back, disengage, and hypercarry protection even when their structures overlap. Hermod should record the chosen interpretation and the known disagreement.

Do not flatten different claims

Four epistemic statuses keep the reasoning honest.

ASSERTED

A source made the claim.

“This composition depends on Rakan finding access.” Recording the assertion does not certify it as true.

DERIVED

A declared rule supports the conclusion.

Rakan’s movement and control can contribute to Target Access when the stated conditions hold.

INTERPRETED

An analyst established strategic meaning.

This recurring pathway belongs to the Protect Hypercarry archetype. Reasonable analysts may disagree.

NORMATIVE

A team declared what should matter.

Preserving Jinx is more important here than retaining another initiation route.

Confidence is separate from status.

A claim can be confidently asserted and still wrong. A derived conclusion can be reproducible while relying on uncertain inputs. An interpretation can be well supported without becoming mechanical fact.

Confirmation is not validation

Search for the example that breaks the clean story.

If we collect only explanations that resemble our vocabulary, the method becomes a way to decorate assumptions with community language.

Reverse the order

Can control establish access?

Long-range control may create the opening before a champion physically reaches the target.

Remove a stage

Is information always required?

Unavoidable or globally available access may not require the information advantage proposed by the candidate pathway.

Find another route

Can pressure substitute for isolation?

A team may force protection away from the target rather than directly prevent reinforcement.

Change the context

Does the relationship survive another patch or rank?

A sequence that is reliable in coordinated play may not be realistic in a different environment.

A counterexample can narrow a definition, introduce a condition, split one pathway into several, or show that the original interpretation should be rejected.

Popularity is context, not truth

What an upvote cannot establish.

It may indicate
  • Visibility
  • Resonance with readers
  • Agreement among people who encountered the post
  • Fit with one community’s expectations
It does not establish
  • Mechanical correctness
  • Causal validity
  • Effectiveness across contexts
  • Representativeness across ranks or regions
  • Community consensus
A popular explanation can be wrong. A valuable minority interpretation can remain obscure.

Votes may be retained as source context, but they should not determine whether a strategic claim is accepted.

Review without pretending to certainty

Validation should be proportional and repeatable.

1

Check the mechanics

Compare mechanical claims with authoritative game information and preserve patch context.

2

Code independently when possible

Ask another analyst to classify the same claims, then record disagreement and adjudication.

3

Review with experienced players

Test whether the abstraction preserves the strategic distinctions practitioners consider meaningful.

4

Test additional compositions

Look for cases that should fit, should not fit, and expose hidden assumptions in the definition.

5

Version the conclusion

Record what changed, why it changed, and which earlier conclusions may no longer hold.

Ethical use of community material

Public discussion is not permission to erase the people who produced it.

Link and distinguish

Connect to sources where appropriate and clearly separate direct quotations, summaries, and Hermod’s interpretation.

Quote minimally

Preserve only what is necessary to support the claim and avoid unnecessary personal identification.

Respect context

Do not treat deleted, private, restricted, or decontextualized material as an unlimited research corpus.

Avoid false consensus

Never present one user, thread, creator, subreddit, rank, or region as the voice of the entire community.

Disclose automation

If automated systems help organize candidate claims, identify that role and require human review for published interpretations.

Preserve disagreement

Do not remove conflicting language merely because it prevents the model from producing a clean category.

What this method cannot guarantee

Traceability is not omniscience.

Our source corpus will always be incomplete. Public voices are not representative of every player. Terminology changes between communities. Interpretations reflect analyst assumptions. Patch changes can invalidate relationships. Agreement does not prove effectiveness, and absence from the model does not imply absence from the game.

A methodology cannot remove those limits. It can prevent us from hiding them and make it possible for someone else to identify where a conclusion should change.

Our methodological position

Show the work. Preserve the disagreement. Keep the model revisable.

Hermod begins with community knowledge, retains its source context, separates claims from interpretations, consolidates recurring relationships into candidate structures, and searches for counterexamples before treating a clean pattern as a useful one.

We should be judged not by whether every abstraction is correct on the first attempt, but by whether its origin is inspectable, its assumptions are explicit, and better evidence can change it.

We are not extracting the correct model from the community. We are building a model the community can examine, challenge, and improve.
