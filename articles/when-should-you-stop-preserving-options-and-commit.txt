# When Should You Stop Preserving Options and Commit?

Your team has two picks left. Several enemy choices remain hidden, and one available champion fills a requirement shared by your remaining lineups. Should you secure that champion now or wait for more information?

The decision is about which choices can safely stay open. You will learn to identify the next useful reveal, check what waiting could make unavailable, and decide what to secure now while preserving a later choice.

## Name the Choice You Are Delaying

Commitment happens at several levels. Selecting a champion, assigning a role, and choosing a fight plan can happen at different times. Securing a requirement shared by several plans may give the remaining picks more freedom.

For this preparation exercise, your team has Gnar Top, Orianna Mid, and Jinx Bottom. It wants grouped objective fights around Jinx. In its practiced setups, Jungle must supply a durable front and a fight start outside Gnar's Mega window.

Jungle and Support are open. Your team must pick once now; the opponent will make its remaining selections before your final pick. There are no bans left.

Your roster review found one acceptable Jungle choice for the planned fight: Sejuani. The alternatives are unavailable or fail a requirement in this scenario. Support still has two practiced completions: one emphasizes protection against access, and another adds ways to force a target against an opponent holding range.

These are assumptions for the exercise. They describe this roster's available completions. The task is to decide which open role to fill first.

## Check What the Reveal Can Change

Start with the decision the missing information could improve. The opponent's remaining selections may reveal whether your final Support should emphasize protection or additional initiation.

That reveal will arrive before your final pick, so you can still use it. Confirm that both Support options remain available in the branches you expect to face. If an enemy choice removes one, the surviving completion must still be acceptable against that response.

Now ask whether the same information would change the Jungle choice. Under the exercise assumptions, Sejuani supplies a requirement needed by every acceptable completion. Learning more about the opponent would refine the Support choice while leaving that Jungle requirement intact.

This gives waiting a specific purpose: preserve the Support decision until the threat is clearer. It also identifies the limit of that benefit. The team gains little by delaying its only acceptable Jungle solution.

## Secure the Shared Requirement

Check what can disappear before the final pick. The opponent still has Jungle open and could select Sejuani during its intervening choices.

If your team selects Support now and loses Sejuani, its final slot has no acceptable way to finish the intended lineup. The information gained during that wait would arrive alongside the loss of the plan it was supposed to improve.

Selecting Sejuani now preserves the two Support completions under the conditions already checked. The team can then use the opponent's reveal to choose between them.

A completed decision sounds like this: “We will secure Sejuani because every acceptable completion needs her and the opponent can take her before our final pick. Support stays open so we can respond to their remaining threats.”

## Change Which Requirement Is Scarce

Now revise the preparation findings. The roster has several acceptable Jungle choices, with different strengths against the opponent's remaining threats. Support has only one acceptable solution to a protection requirement already established by the revealed champions.

Suppose the opponent can take that Support before your final pick, while enough usable Jungle options will survive its remaining selections. The preferred order changes: secure Support now and use the reveal to choose Jungle later.

The reasoning stays consistent across both cases. Protect the requirement at risk and leave the adaptable decision open.

Timing can change the answer again. If a useful enemy reveal arrives after the choice it would affect, that choice must use the evidence already available. If waiting could remove every acceptable completion, first look for a viable pivot; continued delay cannot repair an unavailable lineup.

## Judge the Commitment Threshold

The commitment threshold is the point where the benefit of securing the plan outweighs the useful choices gained by waiting.

The diagram illustrates how that comparison can move as information arrives and options disappear. Its curves are conceptual. A single pick can abruptly change the available completions, and each role or route may reach its threshold at a different time.

Use the worked case to ground the comparison. Waiting on Support can improve a real choice. Waiting on the only acceptable Jungle champion risks a loss the final slot cannot repair. The relevant evidence is the available lineups, the selection order, and the opponent's ability to remove a needed piece.

Player execution belongs in that evidence. A nominal substitute that the roster cannot play provides little protection against losing the preferred champion.

## Revisit the Part That Changes

After securing Sejuani, the team still needs to use the next reveal. If the opponent threatens Jinx's access to the fight, review the protection-focused Support completion. If it can hold range and decline the intended fight, review the completion with additional initiation. Each recommendation depends on the actual champions and the team's ability to carry out the jobs.

The remaining-choice tree shows this narrower decision. A commitment can settle one requirement while leaving a meaningful response available elsewhere.

If the opponent creates a problem neither completion can solve, revise the plan using the picks and roles still available. Early commitment is easier to justify when those adjustments are affordable. A plan that depends on one fragile combination deserves closer scrutiny before its supporting picks are locked.

Before moving to another draft, try changing the exercise's reveal order or removing a Support substitute. Identify which decision would become urgent and which could still wait. The evidence should explain any change in your answer.

## Decide What to Secure and What to Leave Open

At the next commitment point, ask:

1. What could we learn before our next relevant choice?
2. Which acceptable completions could disappear while we wait?
3. What should we secure now, and what can remain open?
