A Hermod definition

The meta is the changing landscape of strategies players consider viable, shaped by game mechanics, execution, interpretation, opponent adaptation, and changes to the game itself.

Our positionThe meta is an evolving system of strategic possibilities, choices, responses, and revisions.
More than a ranking

A tier list shows an outcome. We want to understand what produces it.

When people talk about the meta, they often mean the champions, builds, or compositions currently believed to be strongest. That use is practical, but it leaves the most interesting questions unanswered. Why did those choices become strong? What strategic jobs do they perform? Which conditions make them reliable? What adaptations would reduce their value? What would a patch actually change?

A champion can become popular because of raw numbers, mechanical reliability, role flexibility, synergy with another capability, professional imitation, or an opponent’s inability to punish the plan. Those are different explanations. Treating the meta as a ranked list compresses all of them into the same result.

The meta includes the changing system of reasons that makes certain ways of playing appear viable.

Where the reasoning begins

Abilities are the evidence. Capabilities are the language of comparison.

We do not replace the game with abstractions. We use abstractions so that different implementations can be compared without losing their connection to what champions actually do.

Mechanically primary

Champion abilities ground the claim.

Riot defines the action, targeting rules, range, duration, cooldown, and observable outcome. These facts can change with a patch, and every strategic claim must remain traceable to them.

Without mechanical evidence, capability language becomes an unsupported story.
Analytically primary

Capabilities make different champions comparable.

An ability name belongs to one champion. A capability such as movement, protection, control, or information can appear through many different abilities and remain useful as implementations change.

Without reusable concepts, every patch becomes an unrelated list of champion changes.
Both are necessary.

Abilities ground the model. Capabilities let us reason across champions. Strategic structure explains why the comparison matters.

A concrete example

From one action to a strategic contribution.

Rakan’s contribution to engage can be explained through the smaller mechanical claims that support that strategic description.

Champion actionRakan uses Grand Entrance

He moves to a chosen area and can knock enemies airborne on arrival.

→
Observable effectsPosition changes · enemy action is interrupted

The game produces observable changes that let us inspect the basis for a strategic label.

→
Basic capabilitiesMovement · control

Reusable terms describe the mechanical tools supplied by the action.

→
Required conditionsRange · route · timing · follow-up

The movement and control matter only when Rakan can reach the relevant target and the team can use the opening.

→
Strategic contributionTarget Access · Formation Disruption

Under those conditions, the mechanics may help the team perform a larger strategic job.

The important word is “may.” A knockup contributes to initiation when it reaches the right target, at the right time, with a team able to continue the sequence.

Capabilities are relational

One contribution changes what another contribution can do.

Capabilities interact through effects that can create, preserve, or remove the conditions needed by other capabilities.

1Information

Reveals where a target and its protection are located.

→
2Positioning

Uses that information to approach through a viable route.

→
3Target Access

→
4Control

→
5Concentrated Pressure

A more precise claimCapabilities operate under conditions, and their effects can create or remove conditions for other capabilities.
From mechanics to meaning

Each layer answers a different kind of question.

01Ability and action

What happened?

A champion performed a mechanically defined action.

02Effect

What changed?

Movement, action, health, information, position, or another observable state changed.

03Basic capability

What tool was supplied?

A reusable mechanical vocabulary makes implementations comparable.

04Condition

When does it matter?

Range, timing, geometry, target state, information, and prior setup qualify the contribution.

05Strategic capability

What team-level job can it support?

The mechanic may help establish access, preserve an asset, disrupt formation, concentrate pressure, or convert an advantage.

06Pathway

How do the jobs connect?

Strategic capabilities are ordered by relationships such as enables, requires, precedes, or substitutes for.

07Archetype

What recognizable kind of plan is this?

A community gives recurring strategic structures an interpreted identity and name.

08Objective

Why pursue it?

A team declares the result it values and judges whether the plan is appropriate for achieving it.

Order carries meaning

A pathway connects strengths into a sequence.

A composition does not receive strategic coherence merely because it contains several valuable capabilities. The relationships between the jobs explain how one situation is meant to become another.

ACCESS

Reach the relevant target or space.

→
CONSTRAIN

Limit escape, reinforcement, or response.

→
EXPLOIT

Concentrate pressure during the opening.

→
CONVERT

Turn the advantage into an objective or durable state.

The arrows make it a pathway.

They represent meaningful relations: one job enables another, creates its condition, must precede it, or provides an alternate route. Without those relations, the sequence is only a checklist.

Structure does not name itself

Derive relationships. Establish meanings.

What can be discovered

Recurring operator structure

Several pathways may repeatedly use access, constraint, exploitation, and conversion. Their structural similarity can be made visible.

Evidence can support: “These pathways are organized similarly.”
What must be interpreted

Strategic identity

A community may distinguish pick, dive, and flank strategies even when their abstract sequences overlap. Calling some of them “Pick & Collapse” establishes a shared meaning.

Structure alone cannot force: “These are the same archetype.”

Archetypes are useful because people can recognize, teach, contest, and revise them. They should not be manufactured silently by structural similarity.

Two directions of reasoning

Intent works downward. Evidence works upward.

A strategic idea must meet the mechanical evidence somewhere in the middle.

Strategic design
Objective↓Archetype↓Pathway↓Required strategic capabilities

What do we want, what kind of plan fits, and what must the team be able to do?

Mechanical evidence
Champion action↓Observable effect↓Basic capability + conditions↓Supported strategic capabilities

What can these champions contribute, under which conditions, and why should the contribution count?

The meeting point

A strategy becomes more than an aspiration when required strategic capabilities meet evidence-backed champion contributions.

The opponent is part of the meaning

Exposure makes strategic possibility adversarial.

An enemy capability does not automatically counter an allied strategy. It becomes relevant when it can pressure a relationship on which the strategy depends.

Allied requirementDash-based Target Access

Rakan must cross the route and reach the intended formation.

+
Enemy capabilityDash interruption

Poppy can deny movement through a protected area.

+
Activation conditionsPosition · timing · available cooldown

The denial matters only if Poppy can occupy the route when the access attempt occurs.

→
Strategic exposureThe pathway’s entrance can be pressured.

The relationship is conditional, directional, and open to counterplay.

How the system changes

One meaningful change can alter everything that depends on it.

Not every change transforms the meta. A change matters strategically when its consequences propagate through relationships players rely upon.

Hypothetical patchRakan’s entry becomes less reliable

Range, timing, cooldown, or another parameter changes.

→
Mechanical consequenceAccess conditions become harder

Fewer situations allow the same action to reach the relevant target.

→
Strategic consequenceSome pathways become harder to realize

Plans dependent on that entrance lose a reliable provider.

→
Adaptive consequenceOther providers and plans gain relative value

Players revise picks, execution, and counterplay.

Counterfactual stability matters.

Changing an irrelevant fact should not change the strategic conclusion. Consequences should travel only through relationships that actually depend on the changed fact.

The meta is a feedback system

Players discover strategies and change the environment those strategies face.

1Possibility

Game mechanics and player knowledge make certain strategies conceivable.

→
2Selection

Players choose, practice, and demonstrate some possibilities more than others.

→
3Adaptation

Opponents learn the pathway, contest its conditions, and prioritize counters.

→
4Revision

Players alter providers, sequences, meanings, and strategic commitments.

→
5Game change

Balance changes can reshape the mechanical possibilities and begin another cycle.

A strategy can become weaker without a direct nerf because opponents learn to deny its conditions. A previously ignored capability can become valuable because it answers a newly common pathway. Popularity, effectiveness, interpretation, and counterplay continuously affect one another.

A living model for a living game

The language must remain revisable.

Mechanics change

Riot can alter abilities, items, systems, maps, objectives, and the conditions around play.

Execution changes

Players discover interactions, improve coordination, and make previously difficult possibilities reliable.

Opponents adapt

A pathway’s conditions can become harder because teams learn how to recognize and deny it.

Meanings change

Communities revise archetype boundaries and develop new language for strategies that matter.

A model that cannot accept correction will eventually describe its own assumptions more faithfully than it describes League of Legends.

What we mean by “The Meta”

Not a list. A changing system of strategic possibility.

Champion mechanics provide the evidence. Capabilities provide a reusable language. Conditions determine when contributions matter. Strategic capabilities describe team-level jobs. Pathways connect those jobs into purposeful sequences. Communities interpret recurring structures as archetypes. Objectives express what teams value. Enemy capabilities create exposures that pressure the plan.

Players select, execute, counter, teach, and revise those possibilities. Riot changes the underlying game. The relationships change, the viable strategies change, and the shared expectations of play change with them.

That evolving system is what Hermod means by “The Meta.”
