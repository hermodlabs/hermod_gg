A philosophy of strategic scale

A lane can tell us what is possible between two players. It cannot, by itself, tell us why that possibility matters to this team, in this game, under this strategic environment.

Our positionThe lane tells you what can happen. The larger game tells you why it matters.
The problem with isolated advice

A locally correct action can serve the wrong result.

League advice often stops at the nearest visible contest: win the trade, push the wave, take the matchup, or punish the cooldown. Those observations can be mechanically correct. The mistake is treating local advantage as its own explanation.

Push for what? Preserve health for what? Force the opponent to remain in lane while what happens elsewhere? A decision becomes strategic when we connect the action to a result worth creating and to the other players, timings, and resources required to create it.

We can analyze a lane locally. We cannot determine the full strategic value of a lane decision from the matchup alone.

Four views of the same game

Each scale answers a different question.

These are not ranks of intelligence. Zooming in and zooming out reveal different relationships.

01Micro

What can this player do now?

Spacing, trading, cooldown use, last-hitting, and wave manipulation.

02Meso

What can a small group create together?

A lane pair, gank, roam, skirmish, vision contest, or local numbers advantage.

03Macro

What team-level result does this serve?

Objective access, map pressure, protection, resource allocation, and conversion.

04Meta

Why is this kind of plan valuable here?

The strategies players consider viable under the current patch, conventions, opponents, and adaptations.

“Meta” does not mean a tier list.

It is the changing environment of strategic possibilities, expectations, responses, and revisions in which macro plans are chosen.

The larger view gives direction. The smaller view makes it real.

Repeated adaptation

Changes what players believe is viable.

Strategy is not handed down from an abstract plan to obedient players. Plans constrain actions, actions test plans, and repeated results change the environment in which future plans are chosen.

One action · four meanings

Should the top laner freeze the wave?

“Freezing is good here” is incomplete until the word here has enough context.

Micro

The freeze can deny farm.

The opponent must enter dangerous space, while the allied laner farms nearer safety.

Meso

The freeze can create a gank window.

That matters only if the jungler can reach the lane and the pair can threaten the target together.

Macro

The freeze can cost first movement.

If the team needs top priority for an objective, holding the wave may work against the larger plan.

Meta

The environment changes the exchange.

Objective timing, Teleport rules, lane-swap patterns, and champion incentives affect which result is worth more.

No universal verdict follows.

The example does not prove “freeze” or “push.” It shows which additional facts turn a mechanic into a strategic judgment.

Two ways reasoning breaks

Neither the smallest nor the largest view is sufficient.

Local reduction

“I won lane, so the decision was correct.”

Winning the immediate contest does not establish that the resources, timing, build, or pressure served the composition’s most important need.

Strategic abstraction

“The plan was correct, so execution is secondary.”

A pathway that depends on unavailable range, timing, familiarity, or coordination is not rescued by an elegant strategic description.

How this position shapes Hermod

Let players move between the views.

01

Begin with the decision

Explain the matchup, action, or local interaction the player is trying to understand.

02

Trace what it enables

Connect the local result to nearby cooperation, team capabilities, and strategic pathways.

03

Expose the dependency

Show what must also be true before the local advantage becomes strategically useful.

04

Return to the player

Translate the larger purpose back into an action the player can actually take.

Our position

Zoom out for purpose. Zoom in for agency.

Micro, meso, macro, and meta are different languages for describing one connected game. Good analysis does not choose one and dismiss the others. It shows how the same decision changes meaning as the relevant relationships become visible.

A useful model helps the player see both the action they control and the larger result that makes it worth doing.
