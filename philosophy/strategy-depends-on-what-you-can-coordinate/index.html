A philosophy of agency and coordination

The same five champions create a different strategic problem when intention is shared, uncertain, or unavailable. Organized teams can design a collective plan. Solo Queue players must infer possibilities and choose a useful contribution without pretending to control four strangers.

Our positionUse the same strategic language—but ask only the questions the player has enough agency and evidence to answer.
One game · two decision contexts

Coordination changes the meaning of the problem.

An organized team can discuss what it values, choose a strategic pattern, assign responsibilities, and practice the timing needed to realize a pathway. Its draft can begin with a declared intention because the people making the choices can share that intention.

A Solo Queue player enters a different situation. The other picks are visible, but the reasons behind them usually are not. Communication is limited, familiarity is unknown, and synchronized follow-up cannot be assumed. The player still makes strategic choices, but those choices begin from evidence rather than agreement.

Solo Queue is not strategy without order. It is strategy under partial knowledge and limited control.

Do not collapse the contexts

Shared intention and inferred possibility are different kinds of claim.

Neither context is more legitimate. Each permits different conclusions.

Organized team

“We intend to Protect Hypercarry.”

The players can explicitly choose the objective, archetype, pathway, assignments, and acceptable tradeoffs. Champion selections can be evaluated against that declared promise.

Intent can organize the evidence.
Solo Queue

“These picks support Protect Hypercarry.”

The composition may contain the required capabilities, but structural support does not prove that five independent players recognize or intend the same pathway.

Evidence can reveal possibilities—not shared intent.
The wording matters.

“Supports” is an evidence claim. “Intends” is a claim about people. Software should not silently turn the first into the second.

Begin from available agency

Ask what the person using the tool can actually change.

A useful workflow respects the boundary between collective control and individual choice.

01Organized team

Construct a shared strategy

Choose what matters, compare archetypes, commit to pathways, distribute capability requirements, and revise the composition together.

Objective → archetype → pathway → champions
02Solo Queue

Make a reliable contribution

Observe the current picks, consider the champions available in your role and pool, infer plausible pathways, and choose what you can contribute without requiring hidden agreement.

Visible picks + champion pool → plausible pathways → personal contribution
The concepts remain · the questions change

Four strategic lenses viewed from two positions.

Coverage, realizability, robustness, and optionality remain useful. Their interpretation must follow the user’s actual agency.

LensOrganized teamSolo Queue
Coverage

Does the composition supply the capabilities required by the chosen plan?

What important team needs could my available pick cover?

Realizability

Can this group coordinate the conditions needed to execute the pathway?

Can I produce value without precise cooperation I cannot assume?

Robustness

Does the plan survive counter-pressure or the loss of a critical provider?

Does my contribution remain useful when allies misread the situation or the game becomes disorganized?

Optionality

Which meaningful team pathways remain available after this decision?

How many useful jobs can I still perform as the game develops?

Coordination burden belongs inside realizability.

A pathway may be structurally supported and still be difficult to realize without shared timing, vision, resource allocation, or follow-up.

A concrete support decision

“Who is strongest?” is not the question.

Imagine the allied draft already has damage and initiation but lacks reliable disengagement and protection. The player’s practiced support pool contains Janna, Rakan, and Pyke.

Janna

Adds missing protection

Disengagement and ally preservation address an existing composition need with relatively low dependence on allied initiation timing.

Rakan

Reinforces initiation

Access and initiation may be valuable, but they duplicate a job the composition already supplies and require responsive allied follow-up.

Pyke

Opens another pathway

Pick pressure and conversion create an alternate possibility while increasing dependence on access, tempo, and successful early pressure.

The model should explain the exchange, not declare a universal winner.

Player familiarity, lane matchup, role constraints, patch state, and personal judgment can make a different choice more realizable. The recommendation must remain answerable to those assumptions.

Uncertainty is not a character judgment

Model dependence without rating strangers.

We may represent
  • Limited communication
  • Unconfirmed strategic intent
  • Unknown follow-up timing
  • Unshared practice
  • Matchmade role assignment
We should not infer
  • That teammates are incompetent
  • That a player will refuse cooperation
  • That champion class reveals intent
  • That structural support guarantees execution
  • That one coordination style is morally superior

The unknown should remain unknown. It can qualify confidence without becoming a story about another person.

How this position shapes Hermod

Different assumptions require different tools.

A shared model does not require a shared interface.

01

Infer several plausible plans

Visible picks can support pathway hypotheses. They cannot establish a collective declaration.

02

Respect the player’s champion pool

A theoretically cleaner fit may be less realizable than a familiar champion with a slightly different contribution.

03

Expose coordination dependence

Show when value requires synchronized vision, timing, protection, resource funneling, or rapid follow-up.

04

Give the player a strategic role

Explain the likely job, the conditions it needs, and how that job may change when the original pathway becomes unavailable.

Our position

Do not promise control the player does not have.

Organized teams need tools for constructing and auditing shared intention. Solo Queue players need tools for recognizing plausible team possibilities and choosing a robust personal contribution under uncertainty.

The capabilities, conditions, pathways, and exposures can remain consistent across both contexts. The claims, defaults, and recommendations must change with the coordination available.

Start from what is visible. Respect what is unknown. Recommend only within the player’s agency.
