# Build Backups That Survive Disruption

Blue has one Support slot left, and the opponent can displace the champion protecting Jinx. Should Blue add another protection tool or choose stronger lane pressure? This article shows how to test a substitute against a specific failure and decide whether its protection is worth the cost.

A backup that preserves a needed job after the primary route fails supplies **strategic redundancy**. Its value depends on the disruption it survives and on what the draft gives up to obtain it.

## Name the Failure and the Job to Preserve

Imagine Blue relies on Orianna's space control to keep an enemy approach away from Jinx. Assume the opponent can force Orianna sideways, opening that approach for a diver. Jinx then has to retreat before delivering useful damage.

The backup's job is precise: preserve Jinx's attack window against that diver while Orianna is displaced. Extra initiation or general durability would need a separate argument showing how it performs this job.

Blue is considering a practiced defensive Support whose peel can answer the expected diver. For this example, assume the relevant tool is available and the Support can remain beside Jinx when Orianna is forced away. Those conditions make the candidate worth testing.

## Test Whether the Substitute Survives

Rehearse the displacement. Move Orianna out of position, send the expected diver through the opened approach, and check whether Support can keep Jinx attacking from a safe position.

Under the stated assumptions, the replacement works: it uses a different position and its own available cooldown. The same displacement that removes Orianna's protection leaves this response intact.

## Compare the Protection With the Slot Cost

Assume this Support choice weakens lane pressure compared with Blue's other practiced option. Blue may lose an early rotation and need Mid priority to prepare the objective entrance.

Choose the defensive option if the displacement is likely, losing Jinx's attack window would break the plan, and Blue can cover the setup cost. Here, assume Mid can supply the necessary priority, so the backup earns the slot.

Record both sides of the decision: “Support preserves Jinx's attack window when Orianna is displaced; we accept weaker lane pressure and use Mid priority for the setup.” This gives practice a failure to rehearse and a cost to monitor.

## Withdraw the Claim When the Same Threat Removes Both

Change one condition: the opponent's threat now forces Support away with Orianna. The proposed substitute loses the position it needs, so this pick no longer covers the named failure.

Reconsider the slot. Stronger lane pressure may help Blue establish a safer formation earlier, or another practiced protection route may survive the disruption. Re-run the substitution test.
