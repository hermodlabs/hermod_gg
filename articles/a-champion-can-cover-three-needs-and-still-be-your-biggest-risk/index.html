Balance the Value and Risk of Multi-Function Picks

Find competing responsibilities, choose a priority, and assign a handoff the team can execute.

On this page
  • Put the Responsibilities in the Same Moment
  • Check Position, Timing, and Shared Resources
  • Assign a Priority and an Accepted Handoff
  • Cancel the Handoff When Cover Disappears
  • Resolve the Forced Choice

An engage opportunity appears just as Jinx needs protection. Maokai is assigned both jobs. Which action should he take, and who accepts the other responsibility? This article shows how to detect incompatible assignments, set a priority, and define a handoff the team can execute.

A champion who fills several requirements creates compression value: fewer jobs remain for the other picks. That efficiency holds when the jobs fit the available time, positions, and resources.

Put the Responsibilities in the Same Moment

Imagine Blue has a carry-oriented Top, Maokai Jungle, a scaling control mage Mid, Jinx ADC, and an enchanter Support. Maokai is expected to check vision, establish a front, initiate, and protect Jinx near Dragon. He can check an entrance before the team establishes its formation. The immediate conflict comes later: advancing to initiate places him away from Jinx as an enemy approaches her.

Assume both demands occur together and Maokai has the resources for either action. He still cannot occupy both positions. The responsibility diagram isolates that conflict while every champion remains available.

Check Position, Timing, and Shared Resources

Place the jobs on the same map and timeline. Initiation needs Maokai forward when a target becomes reachable. Protection needs someone beside Jinx when the enemy closes. Check whether both jobs also require the same control ability. Use the compatibility matrix to compare these demands against the actual formation and cooldowns. It helps expose plans that spend one resource twice or allow no time to return.

In this example, assume Support can handle the expected access threat with an available defensive cooldown while staying beside Jinx. That creates a possible division of work. Verify that the roster has practiced it before calling both jobs covered.

Assign a Priority and an Accepted Handoff

Blue's priority is preserving Jinx's attack window. Maokai may advance once Support is in position and confirms responsibility for the immediate threat. Support's acknowledgment is the handoff; an attractive target by itself gives Maokai no assurance that Jinx is covered.

The assignment can be brief: “Support owns Jinx's immediate protection; Maokai initiates after that cover is confirmed.” Maokai can then concentrate on the opening while Support watches the expected approach.

Include the cost. Support's defensive resource is reserved for this job, and Blue may pass up an early initiation while waiting for the handoff. If the expected threat exceeds what Support can handle, the plan needs a different assignment or formation before Maokai commits.

Cancel the Handoff When Cover Disappears

Now suppose Support has already spent the required cooldown. All five champions are alive, but the agreed cover is unavailable. Maokai stays in a position to protect Jinx and lets the offensive opening pass. Blue can reconsider initiation once protection is restored or the enemy access threat ends.

The priority remains the same while the action changes. Rehearse the cancellation signal: “I cannot cover; hold.” A silent assumption can leave both players advancing or both waiting. Apply the same check to simultaneous vision and protection duties: decide who moves, who waits, and when responsibility changes.

Resolve the Forced Choice

  1. Which critical jobs happen together, and do their positions, timing, or required resources conflict?
  2. Which job takes priority when one champion cannot do both?
  3. Who accepts the other job, what confirms the handoff, and what signal cancels it when the required cover disappears?
