A foundation for strategic thinking

When players say a strategy “works,” they may mean four different things. The team may possess the right tools, be able to coordinate them, survive resistance, or retain another way forward. Those claims should not be confused.

Four lensesCoverage asks what we can do. Realizability asks whether we can do it here. Robustness asks whether we can keep doing it when resisted. Optionality asks what else we can do.
Why four questions?

The right champions can still produce the wrong kind of confidence.

A composition can contain every mechanic associated with a plan and still fail to execute it. It can execute beautifully until one opponent interrupts the critical champion. It can survive that interference but have no useful answer when the opponent refuses the expected fight.

Calling all of these situations “good” or “bad” hides the difference between them. The four questions give players a more precise language for describing what kind of possibility a composition actually possesses.

A strategy does not work in only one sense.

The vocabulary

Four ordinary questions, made explicit.

Begin with the question. Measurement and judgment come later.

01Coverage

What can we do?

Does the composition possess plausible tools for the jobs required by the strategy?

02Realizability

Can we actually do it here?

Can these players coordinate those tools under the relevant timing, range, formation, and game-state conditions?

03Robustness

Can we keep doing it when resisted?

Does the strategy continue to function when the opponent interferes or one contribution fails?

04Optionality

What else can we do?

What meaningfully different strategic directions remain if the preferred one becomes unavailable or undesirable?

01
Structural possibility

Coverage

Does the team possess the tools required by the strategy?

The composition appears to cover the intended jobs.

Ornn and Sejuani can occupy space and apply control. Lulu can protect and reposition Jinx. Orianna can shape the area around the formation and add coordinated follow-up. Jinx can provide sustained damage and help convert a won fight.

That gives us evidence that the composition contains plausible contributions to the plan.

What coverage lets us say“The tools appear to exist.”
FrontlineAccess denialProtectionSustained damageConversion
It does not let us say that the tools will arrive at the right time or survive enemy resistance.
The boundary

Coverage establishes capability supply. Successful execution requires additional evidence.

02
Situated possibility

Realizability

Can this team bring the tools together under the actual conditions?

The ingredients remain present even when the sequence is difficult.

Suppose the Jinx player is highly familiar with spacing, but the Lulu player is unfamiliar with the timing of protection abilities. Sejuani engages beyond Orianna’s effective follow-up range, or the team abandons the formation that allows its protection to work.

Nothing disappeared from the champion kits. Coverage remains. What changed is whether this particular team can make the contributions arrive in the right order, range, geometry, and moment.

What realizability asks us to inspect
  • Player familiarity
  • Role and champion fit
  • Timing and sequence
  • Range and geometry
  • Formation and coordination
  • Available game state
No single item is realizability by itself. Familiarity is one input among several.
The boundary

Realizability asks whether these players can create the required relationship in this situation.

03
Resistant possibility

Robustness

What remains when the opponent interferes or something fails?

The enemy can interrupt the relationships your plan requires.

Imagine the opponent drafts Vi and Nocturne to pressure Jinx. If every protective response depends on Lulu, separating her or drawing out one critical cooldown can collapse several parts of the plan together.

If Lulu, Orianna, Ornn, and Sejuani can each interrupt access in different ways, the composition may survive one failed response. The strategy can withstand some failures while remaining vulnerable to other forms of counterplay.

A relationship under pressureJinx remains safe → Jinx sustains damage → the team converts

Vi pressures access

Nocturne pressures information and isolation

The useful question is which allied responses remain independent when one response is denied.
The boundary

Robustness describes the strategy’s ability to continue or recover under resistance, within its remaining limits.

04
Alternative possibility

Optionality

What meaningfully different ways of playing remain?

The opponent may refuse the fight we designed.

If the enemy never enters the front-to-back fight, Ornn and Sejuani may still initiate from range. Orianna may contribute to a coordinated catch. A separated target may allow the team to create an advantage before returning to Jinx’s conversion strength.

These possibilities matter only when the composition can plausibly execute them. Several differently named plans that all collapse with the same unavailable champion are not strong alternatives.

Meaningful alternatives
Protect and sustainPreferred directionLong-range initiationDifferent entranceCatch and convertDifferent opening
Optionality concerns the number and quality of genuinely different directions.
The boundary

Optionality is the availability of strategically meaningful future choices.

Four kinds of possibility

The words describe different claims.

There is a useful progression from tools to execution, resistance, and adaptation. The four ideas provide lenses for judging tradeoffs according to the team’s priorities.

Tradeoffs are the point

Do not average the questions into one answer.

There is no context-free formula that tells every team how much resilience or flexibility should be exchanged for focus.

Neither profile is universally better.

The opponent, the players, the intended strategy, and the tradeoffs the team knowingly accepts determine which shape is appropriate.

A better language game

Move from assigning a property to explaining a relationship.

Vague property

“This is a good Jinx composition.”

→
Inspectable explanation

“The composition covers the jobs needed to protect Jinx. It is realizable if we maintain formation and coordinate protection. It is robust because several champions can interrupt access to her. If the enemy refuses that fight, Ornn and Sejuani preserve another way to start.”

The second statement may still be wrong. Its advantage is that players can locate the disagreement: the supplied job, the required condition, the claimed resilience, or the proposed alternative.

Our position

Make strategic possibility inspectable.

Coverage, realizability, robustness, and optionality are not four labels for the same idea. They protect us from claiming that tools guarantee execution, execution guarantees survival, or one working plan guarantees the ability to adapt.

They do not remove judgment. They give players a clearer object about which to judge, disagree, and learn.

A strategy can be supported, executable, resilient, and adaptable. Each claim deserves its own reason.
