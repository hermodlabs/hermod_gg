A philosophy of qualified advice

Players deserve useful answers before every uncertainty is resolved. They also deserve to know when an answer changes because the rest of the draft, the map, or the team’s purpose changes.

Our positionStart with the context the player has. Strengthen the recommendation as more of the game becomes known.
Advice always contains an objective

“Best” is an unfinished sentence.

A champion can be best for surviving lane, gaining priority, supplying engage, protecting a carry, preserving several team options, or exploiting a particular opponent. Those are different problems. A recommendation that does not name its objective hides the judgment that produced it.

This does not mean every player must describe a complete team strategy before receiving help. It means the answer should be no more confident or comprehensive than the evidence permits.

Give the smallest useful answer now. Show what additional context could change it.

Progressive strategic context

The analysis should grow with the draft.

Each layer preserves the earlier facts while changing what we can responsibly conclude from them.

This is not “complete the form before we help.”

A partial draft supports partial conclusions. Unknown picks and intentions should remain visible uncertainty, not empty fields silently treated as facts.

How an answer changes

“Should I push this wave?”

With matchup context

You can probably push safely.

Your champion has the wave control and trading position to gain priority against this lane opponent.

Establishes feasibility—not purpose.
With nearby-player context

Pushing may enable the river contest.

Your jungler is positioned to use first movement, while the opposing jungler’s location remains uncertain.

Adds a local use and an exposure.
With composition context

Preserving the wave may serve the team better.

Your team is not prepared to contest now and benefits more from avoiding the opponent’s preferred early fight.

Changes the objective being optimized.

The first answer was not necessarily false. It answered a smaller question. The error would be presenting it as the complete strategic answer.

Do not fill uncertainty with fiction

We cannot know what four strangers intend.

Solo Queue analysis should infer possibilities from visible evidence without converting those possibilities into a story about other players.

We can observe
  • Selected champions and likely role eligibility
  • Mechanics and modeled capability contributions
  • Current position, resources, cooldowns, and objectives when supplied
  • Which pathways the visible composition could support
We should not pretend to know
  • That teammates share one strategic intention
  • That a player will execute an unfamiliar job
  • That an enemy will choose the expected response
  • That a possible pathway will be recognized or coordinated
The practical response to uncertainty

Preserve useful choices instead of predicting hidden intentions.

What we can examineWhich meaningful options remain available

A flexible pick is not automatically better. It may trade focused strength for several credible contributions. The point is to make that exchange visible: which current need does the pick solve, which alternative pathways remain, and what conditions would make each option usable?

Optionality is not indecision.

It is the capacity to respond when the first assumption fails. Once the situation becomes clearer, deliberate commitment may be the better choice.

How this position shapes Hermod

Every recommendation should carry its scope.

01

Name the question answered

Distinguish “good into this lane” from “good for this composition.”

02

Show the evidence used

Make visible which picks, capabilities, conditions, and objectives affected the result.

03

Show what could change it

Identify unknown enemy picks, coordination requirements, or strategic tradeoffs that qualify the advice.

04

Offer branches, not an oracle

Explain the credible options and let the player choose according to familiarity, judgment, and purpose.

Our position

Context should improve advice without becoming a toll gate.

Hermod should be useful when the player knows only the lane and more useful when the composition becomes visible. A completed draft gives the analysis more to compare; it does not retroactively make every earlier local observation worthless.

Answer what the evidence supports. Qualify what remains uncertain. Revise when the context changes.
